On average people spend over 219$ dollars a month on subscriptions to streaming services. Most of us have be accustomed to the idea of renting our digital lives rather than owning it. I can bet that most people are subscribed to a multitude of the subscriptions bellow:

- Music Streaming Subscriptions
- Video Streaming Subscriptions
- Book Subscriptions

It has become normalized to spend money monthly on services that we may use only a handful of times. The multitude of ways I convinced myself I needed to pay for these subscriptions was laughable. Some of my reasons included:

- Spotify: "I need the music algorithm..."
- Neflix: "I need to see this show the moment it comes out..."
- Gamepass: "Halo is coming out and I want it to be FREE..."
- Youtube: "I don't want to see adds..."

Some of the reasons are valid while others straight delusional. In reality we don't need any of these subscriptions. We can do the following instead:

- Spotify - Buy music CD(s) or buy digital files
- Netflix - Buy Bluerays or digital files
- Gamepass - Buy your games 
- Youtube - Use an add blocker or just don't watch it

These alternatives are simple and substantially cheaper in the long run. We convince ourselves that paying for subscriptions is cheaper because we gain access to vast libraries of content for a small monthly fee. If you are like me the reality is that on average we don't maximize the value we are paying for. Let's see a few examples: 

*Spotify*:
On average I add maybe 3-5 songs a month to my playlist. Sometimes I don't even add music every month in fact that is usually the case. I have curated a massive library of music on Spotify that I basically just listen to on shuffle. A Spotify subscription is around 12 dollars including taxes on month. That's $144.00 a year. A song costs $0.50 to $2.00. So if I buy 5 songs at $1.50 a piece every two months that's $45.00 a year and I own them forever. Even if I spend up to $144.00 worth of songs that year I'm still getting more value because I own them permanently. If one year I don't get new music I spend $0.00 that year.
